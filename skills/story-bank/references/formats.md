# Story formats

## STAR (execution-level questions)
- **Situation**: the setting and stakes in one or two sentences.
- **Task**: what the user specifically owned.
- **Action**: the steps the user took, in order, with "I" for their own decisions.
- **Result**: the measurable outcome, only numbers that exist in the source, plus what happened next.

## CLEAR (leadership-level alternative to STAR)
- **Context**: the macro pressure behind the work (market shift, reorg, financial risk, organizational stakes), not a minor team conflict.
- **Learning**: the diagnosis before acting. What the user listened for and found: root cause, stakeholder insight, or a gap in their own knowledge.
- **Execution**: the decisions and how the user led: alignment, delegation, change management, resource allocation. "I" for strategic choices, "we" for operational execution.
- **Achievement**: the measurable business outcome (revenue, efficiency %, retention, time-to-market, engagement scores). Numbers only where they exist, never invented.
- **Review**: what lasted and scaled. The framework, tool, or process that outlived the user's direct involvement, and what they would do next.

## Choosing
STAR fits "tell me about a time you…" execution questions. CLEAR fits strategy, leadership, influence-without-authority, and change questions. Most strong stories can be told both ways; keep both in the bank.
